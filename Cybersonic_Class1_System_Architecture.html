02Image

The Big Picture

  • 1
    ClientThe phone, tablet, or laptop you're holding right now.The device that initiates a request — a browser, an app, anything with a UI.
  • 2
    NetworkThe path your request travels — through Wi-Fi, cables, and towers — to get there.The layers of infrastructure (Wi-Fi, routers, ISPs) your request passes through, addressed by IP.
  • 3
    ServerA powerful computer, somewhere else, that's always on and waiting to help.A always-on machine that receives the request, runs logic, and decides what to send back.
  • 4
    DatabaseWhere the server keeps everything it needs to remember, neatly organized.Structured storage the server reads from and writes to — tables, rows, and relationships.
03Illustration

The Restaurant Analogy

The Postal Service Analogy

  • 1
    You orderYou (the client) tell the waiter what you want — that's a request.
  • 2
    Waiter carries itThe waiter walks your order to the kitchen — that's the network.
  • 3
    Kitchen cooksThe kitchen (server) makes the food, checking the pantry (database) for ingredients.
  • 4
    Food arrivesThe waiter brings your food back — that's the response.
  • 1
    Application layerYou write the letter — the actual message, like "GET this webpage."
  • 2
    Transport layerYou address the envelope to a specific room number — that's a port, making sure it reaches the right program.
  • 3
    Internet layerThe postal service routes it using the street address — that's the IP address, guiding it across networks.
  • 4
    Physical layerThe actual trucks, planes, and cables that physically carry it there.

Same shape, every time you open an app: ask → carry → make → return.

Every request you send is wrapped in layers like this — from the message down to the physical wire.
